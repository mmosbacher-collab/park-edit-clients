01Product Overview

The Park Edit is a personalised Disney park planning tool delivered as a mobile-optimised HTML file hosted on a dedicated domain. Each client receives a bespoke day-by-day guide built around their specific family, parks, dates, and pre-booked reservations.

The product is positioned as an antidote to family vacation pressure. The tone is factual, friendly, reassuring, and solution-oriented. Clients should never feel they must do anything or that fun is mandatory.

Two Deliverables

1. The Park Edit (this product)
The in-park day guide. Used on the day, in the park, on a phone. Boarding pass UI per day, timeline schedule, ride detail drawers, SOS menu, In-Park Intel tips.
2. The Pre-Edit
A separate deliverable covering everything before the gates open — Lightning Lane strategy, Early Entry, Memory Maker, rider swap, packing, resort logistics, and family-specific planning. Delivered as a mobile-optimised HTML file at theparkedit.com/[familyname]/pre-edit. Built and in active use.

Client Scope

Primarily families with young kids, but intentionally left open. Most trips are multi-park but some clients may do one park only — never pressure to do more. Philosophy is doing the exciting things well, not hitting everything.

02Tone & Voice

The Park Edit sounds like a knowledgeable friend talking casually — not a guide giving instructions, not a travel blog, not a Disney manual.

Factual
Lead with useful information. Explain the why once and move on. Don't repeat or over-explain.
Friendly
Casual register. "You can" not "you must." Conversational asides are fine — "for whatever reason," "totally fine either way."
Reassuring
Honest about variability. Acknowledge when things depend on the day or the cast member. Solutions first.
Solution-oriented
When something goes wrong, lead with what to do. No preaching, no "I told you so" framing.

What to Avoid

Stakes language
No "this is the most important moment," "don't miss this," "priority #1." Let the plan speak for itself.
Directives
No "go straight here," "no detours," "you must." Always "you can" or just describe what's available.
Predicting reactions
Never predict how specific kids will react. "Rowe is going to love this" sets an expectation the day might not deliver. Exception: "Rowe is going to want to compare scores" is approved — it's observation not prediction.
Pressure to have fun
The product is an antidote to vacation pressure. Never make clients feel they must do anything or that missing something is a failure.
"We recommend"
Always "I" or direct address. This is one person talking to a client, not a company.

The Voice in Practice

The four pillars above describe the what. This section describes the how.

Specificity over generality
Every useful detail earns its place. Not "there's seating upstairs" — "go upstairs if you can, it's noticeably quieter." Not "the queue is entertaining" — "the queue itself is genuinely entertaining for a Snow White fan." The detail is always specific and always useful. Vague details are worse than no details.
Rhythm
Sentences vary in length deliberately. A longer sentence carrying information, followed by a short one landing it. Copy never reads like a bulleted list even when it technically is one. Read it aloud — if it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it.
One personality moment per section
Warmth comes through in one small specific moment, not spread across everything. "Loud and chaotic in the best way." "Dare I say, pleasant." "You'll want photos of them approximately every four minutes." One line that sounds like a real person, then back to being useful. If every sentence has personality, none of them do.
Restraint
Say the thing once and stop. "Naples is about 3.5 hours." Nothing after that. If you find yourself adding a second sentence to explain the first, the first sentence wasn't clear enough — rewrite it, don't pad it.
Trust the reader
Never explain what they can figure out. Never repeat something already said. The client is an intelligent adult who hired someone to make their trip easier, not someone who needs everything spelled out. Write accordingly.
Earned opinions
Opinions appear when they're genuinely useful — "one of the better quick-service options in Magic Kingdom," "it's unlike anything else in the park." Never superlatives for their own sake, never marketing language. If you wouldn't say it to a friend, don't write it here.

Sentence-Level Rules Updated

These come from copy editing sessions. They're patterns that kept appearing and are now codified.

Active over passive
"You'll ride back to Main Street" not "ride back to Main Street." Active voice puts the reader in motion. Passive voice makes instructions feel like instructions.
No em dashes
Em dashes are banned. Restructure the sentence, use a period, or use a semicolon. The spec used them freely in early versions — they've been consistently edited out in client copy because they interrupt the flow without adding anything useful.
Lead with the experience, follow with logistics
Feeling first, details second. "A real steam-powered train that circles all of Magic Kingdom. Board at Fantasyland — strollers must be folded." Not "Strollers must be folded to board this steam train." The experience earns the logistics.
Signal reasoning with "so"
Use "so" before consequences: "…so have it collapsed before you reach the front of the line." Not bare instructions: "Collapse it before you reach the front of the line." The "so" signals causality and softens the direction into a natural consequence.
Don't draw conclusions for the reader
Cut editorializing. "Good decompression," "everyone sits, nobody walks," "this is perfect for tired legs" — these tell the reader what to think. Let the information do that work. Describe the thing; let them draw the conclusion.
No patronizing parent copy New · v5.5
Do not tell parents things they already know about their own children: how to use a carrier, that toddlers get tired, when to go back to the hotel, what their child can handle. Flag any copy that assumes the client needs coaching on basic parenting. The benchmark: would this sentence feel condescending from a friend who knows you well? If yes, cut it. Relevant audit: removed from Mosbacher Pre-Edit — babywearing heat warning, "dining with little ones" coaching paragraph, "not for every meal" table service framing.
American English only
All client-facing copy uses American English spelling. Common offenders to watch: organized (not organized), recognize (not recognize), color (not colour), traveling (not traveling), canceled (not canceled), favorite (not favorite). The spec itself may contain British spellings in internal or CSS sections — those are acceptable internally but must never appear in client deliverables.
Cause and effect over instruction
Explain why, not just what. "The wind and bubble effects tend to get a big reaction from kids Sam's age: just a heads up so it doesn't catch anyone off guard." Not "be prepared for the bubble effects." The cause (effects happen, kids react) earns the effect (heads up). Instructions without reasoning feel like orders.

18Copy System

The Park Edit uses a structured copy management system to ensure improvements made during one client engagement carry forward to all current and future deliverables. All copy changes are logged, reviewed weekly, and synced to client files via a Python script.

The Workflow

Step 1 — Log
Open the Copy Logger (bookmarked Claude conversation) on your phone. Enter the exact current copy phrase you want to replace, your improved version, a category (optional), and which deliverable it applies to (Park Widget, Prep Guide, or Both). Hit Log. The idea saves locally and syncs to the Google Sheet automatically.
Step 2 — Review (Friday 1:00 PM CST)
A reminder fires every Friday at 1 PM CST. Open the Copy Logger. Review all Pending ideas. Approve the ones worth keeping. Reject the ones that aren't. Approved ideas move to the Approved column in the Google Sheet. Edit categories at this stage if the original tag was incomplete or incorrect.
Step 3 — Apply
For each Approved idea, check which clients need the update (current clients in progress, future clients only, or both). Click Mark as Applied. Then run the Python sync script to push the changes to your HTML files automatically.
Step 4 — Sync
The Python script (park_edit_sync.py) searches each client HTML file for the exact current copy phrase and replaces it with the new version. If the phrase is not found in a file (version inconsistency), it flags that file for manual review rather than skipping silently. A timestamped backup is created before every file is touched.

Copy Categories

Each idea is tagged with one or more categories. Categories can be assigned at log time or edited during Friday review. A single idea can belong to multiple categories if it applies to more than one section of a deliverable.

Park Widget categories: Lightning Lane, Park Strategy, Rope Drop, Midday Break, Fireworks / Evening, Character Meets, Height / Ride Details. Pre-Edit categories: Dining Reservations, MDE Setup, Packing List, Pre-Edit: General, Pre-Edit: Hotel, Pre-Edit: Tickets, Pre-Edit: Baby & Toddler. Shared: General Copy.

Master Copy vs. Client-Specific Copy

Master Copy Improvement
An improvement that applies to all clients going forward. When applied, the sync script updates all flagged client files and the canonical text in this spec is updated manually to reflect the new version. Future clients receive the improved copy automatically.
Client-Specific Copy
Copy written for a specific client's family situation (height restrictions, dietary needs, toddler logistics, etc.). Stays with that client only. Not synced to other files. Tagged "Future Clients Only" if it reveals a pattern worth building into the master copy framework.

Python Sync Script Setup

The sync script (park_edit_sync.py) runs as a local server on your computer and is called by the Copy Logger app when you click Update Files.

One-Time Setup

pip install flask flask-cors gspread google-auth requests
python park_edit_sync.py

The script starts a local server on port 7432. Keep the terminal window open while using the Copy Logger. On your new computer (June), copy the script file and run the same commands — update file paths in the config if your folder structure changes.

File paths are stored in park_edit_config.json in the same folder as the script. The config is also synced to the Google Sheet so it persists across devices. File path convention:

Flagged Files

If the sync script cannot find the current copy phrase in a file, it flags that file rather than skipping silently. Flagged files appear in the sync output with a note: "May be a version inconsistency — check manually." This means the file is using an older or different version of that copy. Open the file, find the relevant section, and update it manually. Then note the inconsistency in the canonical spec so it does not recur.

Backups

Before touching any file, the script creates a timestamped backup in a backups/ folder next to the script. Backups are named YYYYMMDD_HHMMSS_filename.html. To roll back, replace the client file with the backup version.

Community-observed pattern (not published by Disney): significant Lightning Lane inventory is released mid-day. The largest drop is typically around 1:02pm, with a second drop around 3:02pm. The 1:02pm drop often includes Tier 1 rides that were sold out since morning. Useful for clients who miss their morning window or arrive mid-day. Not guaranteed — pattern varies by park, day, and crowd level. Position as a recovery move, not a primary strategy. Use for: all clients using Lightning Lane Multi Pass.
Modify-time button and countdown clock New · v5.5
Two ways to get here. Path 1 — immediately after booking: a modify-time button appears on the confirmation screen. Tap it to search for earlier times without canceling the existing booking. Path 2 — any time after booking: main menu → tap the Lightning Lanes button in the upper right → tap the specific booking you want to change → choose Modify from the menu that appears. Either path lands you on the same modify screen.

On the modify screen, the ride you are modifying is pinned to the top (same pinning mechanic as the Tip Board). Other rides you could switch to appear below it. To refresh available times: close the screen entirely and reopen it. Each open is a fresh look at what is available — unlike the Tip Board where you pull down to refresh.

Critical: a countdown clock runs at the top of the screen. If it expires before you confirm a new time, the time shown disappears and your original booking is kept. Confirm before the clock runs out.

Most effective earlier in the day when LL inventory is actively turning over. Once most Lightning Lanes are claimed, fewer times will surface here. Use for: all clients using Lightning Lane Multi Pass.
Party pre-selection: remove one step from every attempt New · v5.5
When booking a hard ride directly, every attempt requires selecting your party before confirming. If the ride disappears before you finish, the next attempt starts over — party selection included. On a ride like Slinky Dog that may appear and vanish many times before you land it, that step repeats on every single attempt.

The workaround: book any easy available ride first, selecting the exact people you want on the hard ride. That attaches the party to the booking. Then immediately modify that booking. Now every attempt at the hard ride skips the party selection entirely — see a time, tap, confirm. One step instead of two, for every attempt in the session, not just one.

The time saved per attempt is small. The value is that it compounds across however many attempts it takes to land a competitive ride. Use for: clients chasing high-demand rides at Hollywood Studios and Magic Kingdom, especially on busy days.
Experience Redemption (ride breakdown) New · v5.5
The in-app name is "experience redemption" — not Multiple Experiences Pass, which is an older term. When a Lightning Lane attraction breaks down during a guest's return window, the LL converts to an experience redemption automatically. This covers two options: (1) a selection of other rides the guest can use it on instead, or (2) the original ride later in the day once it reopens. Most guests do not know either option exists and walk away assuming the Lightning Lane is lost. If a ride goes down during the client's window, they should check the app before leaving the area — the experience redemption should appear there. Speaking to a cast member at the attraction is also an option if it does not appear automatically. Use for: all clients using Lightning Lane Multi Pass. Worth a sentence in the Pre-Edit LL drawer so clients know it exists before they need it.
Edit Genie selections mid-day New · v5.5
Once a pinned target ride is booked, it should come off the pinned list so the next most competitive ride moves to the top. There is an "edit selections" button in the Genie Tip Board view. Tap it to swap out rides already booked for new targets. Repeat throughout the day as the target list changes. Keeping only the current targets pinned — not a growing list of everything — is what makes the pinning useful rather than just adding scroll length. Use for: clients using Genie pinning on a multi-ride day.
Tap back, tap in again — best available time refresh New · v5.5
Different from the modify-time button. Use this when a ride still has times showing and you want the best available slot rather than a specific time. Tap any showing time → land on the confirmation screen → if the time is not ideal, tap back (do not refresh the view) → tap in again. Each re-entry polls for the best current available time, which may show a different or earlier slot than the previous tap. Repeat until a good time shows. If you see a time you want, you are already on the confirmation screen — no urgency to rush. Best used mid-morning when inventory is still moving. Less effective late in the day when few times remain. Use for: all clients using Lightning Lane Multi Pass, particularly for backing up times mid-day.
Grace periods: beginning, end, and slot release New · v5.5
Two grace periods exist on every Lightning Lane. Beginning: 5 minutes before the window opens. Tapping in during this window glows green and works; tapping in earlier glows blue and requires waiting. End: officially 15 minutes after expiration per Disney. Observed in practice: up to approximately 2 hours after expiration the tap point still glows green. Critical slot mechanic: the booking slot frees up at expiration, not when the ride is used. A new Lightning Lane can be booked in that slot the moment it expires — while the expired booking is still usable for the ~2-hour window. Use for: all clients — the slot-frees-at-expiration detail is important for anyone trying to maximize bookings in a day.
Park hopper: tap between park icons, plan for next park New · v5.5
Once unlocked for park hopping, pull-to-refresh in the Tip Board defaults back to Magic Kingdom every time regardless of current park. Better approach: tap between the park icon tabs at the top of the booking screen instead of pulling to refresh. Each tap checks availability in that park with pinned rides showing at top. Pair with the "plan for next park" setup before hopping: main menu → My Plans → scroll past current plans → tap the button to plan for your next park → select upcoming park and enter target rides. Both park pin lists then show when toggling between park icons, so each tab tap is checking both parks simultaneously. Use for: clients with park hopper tickets.
Last 30 minutes: LL bookings close New · v5.5
The Lightning Lane system stops accepting new bookings 30 minutes before park close. Existing bookings can still be used until the park closes, but no new selections can be made in that window. The last productive push for chasing new rides is approximately 45 to 60 minutes before close. Plan the evening accordingly — anything still needed should be targeted before that window. Use for: all clients using Lightning Lane Multi Pass on a full park day.
Down rides: don't chase New · v5.5
When a ride is closed, Disney holds LL inventory for it because the return-to-service time is unknown. No amount of refreshing will surface times for a downed ride. Far better odds chasing rides confirmed to be running. If a high-priority ride is down, redirect effort to the next target and check back after the ride reopens — inventory often releases again once it's confirmed running. Use for: all clients using Lightning Lane Multi Pass.

Conditional drawer content — hiding logic placement
Conditional content inside drawer bodies (transport blocks, resort-type variants, any content hidden by JS) must have its hiding logic run INSIDE the openDrawer() function, immediately after dBody.innerHTML = data.body. Do NOT put hiding logic in the page-load IIFE. Drawer body HTML is injected dynamically — elements do not exist in the DOM until the drawer opens. Pre-Edit · Family Logistics

Groceries
Order groceries to the resort before you arrive. Check whether your resort has a kitchen — breakfast in the room every morning saves money and sidesteps the food court chaos at 7am before rope drop. Having a few meals in the room breaks up the cost and the chaos of eating out for every meal. Order through Instacart or Amazon Fresh and schedule delivery for check-in day. Bell services accepts the delivery and holds everything refrigerated until your room is ready. You can bring any food into Disney parks — no restrictions. Pack more than you think you need. Use for: all clients, especially those staying at DVC resorts with kitchens.
Babysitters
In-room babysitting is available at Walt Disney World. The main provider is Kid's Nite Out. Sitters are background-checked, experienced with Disney families, and come to your resort room. They bring activities and can handle bedtime. Expect $20–25 per hour plus a booking fee. Although Disney World is for the kids, it can be nice to take a break and have dinner at California Grill. Book before you go — availability fills on busy weeks. Use for: families wanting a date night or break evening.
If you get separated
Lead with preparation, not reaction — this is a planning document. Order: (1) preparation steps (marker/Safety Tat, AirTag wristband, morning photo), (2) Talk to child callout (find a Disney uniform, say your name, practice at home), (3) Park Edit SOS reference, (4) where lost children go (under 10 → Baby Care Center, 10+ → Guest Relations, radio description goes out immediately, stay with the Cast Member). Do not lead with reassurance. The client needs to take action before the trip, not feel better about a hypothetical. Use for: all families with children.
The midday break
Disney parks are immersive precisely because there are no clocks. But bodies need rest. A midday break back at the resort from roughly 1–4pm lets everyone recharge, eat without chaos, and return for the evening shift with actual energy. The break doesn't disrupt your Lightning Lane bookings — you can book both morning and evening parks on the same day. A quiet afternoon is worth more than burning out by 6pm. Use for: families with young children, families staying on property, trips with midday resort access.
Beyond the rides
Disney parks are not solely about rides. Character meets, shopping, eating, watching street performers, sitting and people-watching, taking photos — this is where memory happens. Make room for it in your day. If you're only chasing attractions, you'll hit everything and feel empty. If you stop to enjoy your kids' faces when they meet a character or watch a show, that's the trip. Use for: all clients, especially first-time families and those with young children.
Keeping kids entertained in lines
Even short waits feel endless to kids. A small activity or toy for queue time — not a screen — helps tremendously. Sticker books, a small figurine, a notepad for drawing. Ask cast members for a FastPass reprint (even if you don't need it) so kids have something to interact with while waiting. Many attractions have pre-show areas; let kids explore those rather than rushing through. The wait is part of the experience, not something to survive. Use for: families with children under 8.

Pre-Edit · Baby & Toddler

Baby Care Centers
Every Disney park has one. AC, free, no reservation. Changing tables across all locations are notably nice. Nursing rooms, bottle warmers and microwave, filtered water, high chairs, and a seating area with Disney movies. Good option for kids with sensory sensitivities who need a break. Basic baby supplies for sale (diapers, wipes, formula, children's medicine) — treat as safety net, not supply run, prices are high.

Per-park nursing rooms: MK — shared space, not completely private, rocking chairs, low lights, outlets. Center is small; strollers must be parked outside. EPCOT — larger than MK, slightly less warm in decoration. DHS — very small, may have a wait; nursing room is private but fits one person, so may also have a wait. AK — most spacious, two private nursing rooms with rockers.

Locations: MK near Casey's Corner on Main Street. EPCOT at the Odyssey Pavilion near World Showcase entrance. DHS in the Guest Services building at the entrance. AK near Creature Comforts — go left of the DVC booth and follow the path to First Aid and Baby Care Center. Use for: clients with infants under 3.
Dining with little ones
Quick service is easier with toddlers than table service — you're faster and less worried about making noise. For table service with young kids, go early (lunch before 11:30am, dinner before 5:30pm). Book buffet or family-style restaurants if possible — less waiting on individual plates. Never use dining reservations as a break — it's another activity with an end time. Many restaurants provide high chairs and can seat your party quickly if you ask. Use for: clients with children under 5.
Stroller parking
Stroller parking is free and available outside every major attraction. Your stroller won't be stolen — Disney's lot system means you'll get yours back. Skip the double stroller unless you're bringing a younger sibling along; single strollers fit everywhere. Rent from Disney or bring your own. If you're renting, pick it up early and return by park close — late returns incur fees. Consider a compact umbrella stroller instead for half-days or light walking. Use for: families with toddlers under 3.
Babywearing in the parks
Many rides are easier with a baby in a carrier than pushing a stroller — shorter security lines, easier transitions between attractions. Structured carriers (Ergobaby, LÍLLÉbaby) work well; soft wraps can get hot in Florida. Be aware: some rides ask you to remove carriers for safety. You'll want to bring the carrier even if you're also renting a stroller — having both options makes the day more flexible. Use for: families with infants, families preferring carriers to strollers.

08PWA & Hosting New · v3.0

The Park Edit is delivered as a Progressive Web App (PWA) hosted on a dedicated domain. Clients receive a URL, open it in Safari, and are prompted to save it to their home screen — after which it launches full-screen with no browser chrome, identical to a native app.

What a PWA Is

In plain terms
A website that tells the phone "treat me like an app." Once a client saves it to their home screen it launches full-screen — no URL bar, no Safari controls, no tab strip. The product icon appears on their home screen alongside native apps. After the first load the service worker caches everything, so it works in the parks with no signal.
Why this matters for the product
The browser chrome undercuts the bespoke feel. Full-screen PWA mode removes it entirely. The client's experience is purely The Park Edit — nothing else competing for attention.

Files Required on the Server

index.html
The client's Park Edit file. Each client gets their own folder — e.g. theparkedit.com/salamack/index.html
manifest.json
Tells the phone the app name, icon, colours, and launch behaviour. Can be shared from root across all client folders.
sw.js
Service worker. Caches the app on first load for offline use. Shared from root.
icons/icon-192.png
App icon · 192×192px. Required by Android and some browsers.
icons/icon-512.png
App icon · 512×512px. Required by PWA spec. This is the primary icon.

HTML Head Tags to Add

These lines go immediately after the existing <meta viewport> tag in each client's HTML file:

<meta name="apple-mobile-web-app-capable" content="yes">
<meta name="apple-mobile-web-app-status-bar-style" content="default">
<meta name="apple-mobile-web-app-title" content="Park Edit">
<link rel="apple-touch-icon" href="/icons/icon-192.png">
<link rel="manifest" href="/manifest.json">
Manifest scope: must be "/" Updated · v5.1
The manifest.json lives at the root and serves all client folders. Its scope and start_url must both be set to "/" — never to a client-specific path like "/salamack/". A client-specific scope causes iOS to reject the manifest for any other folder, resulting in a gray box placeholder instead of the product icon on the Add to Home Screen preview. One shared manifest, root scope, works for every client.
theme-color is set to cream
#FDF8F4 — matches the app header so the iOS status bar above the app blends seamlessly. No visible seam between the status bar and the product.
Manifest link removal — client-specific override New · v5.3
In some cases the automatic PWA install prompt behaviour is undesirable for a specific client — for example, when the client is sharing a file URL that shouldn't trigger an install flow. In these cases, remove the <link rel="manifest"> tag from that client's HTML head. The manual "Save to your home screen" prompt JS block remains and still works without the manifest link — it's triggered by a timer, not the manifest. The shell always includes the manifest link by default. Removal is a per-client decision at delivery time, not a template default.

21Automation Script New · v5.0

Goal

A Node.js script that takes a client intake JSON and outputs a fully populated Park Edit HTML file. Ride, character, and show copy is pulled verbatim from park-edit-library-v2.md. Client-specific layers (rider swap, height flags, LL notes, celebration callouts, logistics copy) are injected programmatically. No copy is written by hand in the HTML output file.

Run command: node generate-park-edit.js path/to/intake.json → outputs a named, deployable HTML file.

Why This Exists

Problem the script solves
Manual builds require Claude to write ride copy from memory under pressure. This causes copy drift — rewrites that deviate from the vetted library voice. The Mosbacher build (May 2026) surfaced this: Remy's Ratatouille Adventure was rewritten entirely instead of pulled from the library. The script eliminates this risk by treating the library as a required source file, not a reference.
First automated build target
Santagati/Salamack. They are the most complex file built to date and the best stress test. Backfill their intake JSON from the existing hand-built file before running the generator.

Session Plan

Four focused sessions. Each has a discrete deliverable that can be tested in isolation before the next session starts. Use Sonnet for all four — this is structured code generation, not reasoning-heavy work.

Session 1 · Library Parser

Parse park-edit-library-v2.md into a structured JavaScript object the generator can query.

Required fields per entry
title, body (HTML string), tags (array), heightReq (number or null), motionWarning (boolean), riderSwap (boolean), llType ('multi' | 'single' | null), icon (emoji), park ('MK' | 'EP' | 'DHS' | 'AK'), type ('ride' | 'character' | 'show' | 'trail'), id (slug key matching activity object keys)
Placeholder handling policy
Entries marked as placeholders (Test Track, Frozen Ever After, Soarin' Across America, Rock 'n' Roller Coaster Starring The Muppets) should cause the parser to emit a warning to stdout but not crash. 22Build Protocol New · v5.3

The build protocol defines the order of operations for every client Park Edit, from intake to delivery. It separates what must be true before the file opens (Gate 1) from what must be true before the file ships (Gate 2). The build lives between those two gates.

Gate 1: Ready to Build

All of the following must be confirmed before the file opens. If any item is missing, the build does not start.

Intake complete
Intake JSON exported and pre-build checklist passed with no errors. All required fields populated. Park days, heights, hotel, and ticket type confirmed.
LL strategy decided
The Lightning Lane strategy is agreed before the file opens. Which rides get Multi Pass. Which get Single Pass. Which throwaway unlocks tier access. Actual booking confirmations are not required at Gate 1 — the window may not have opened yet. Strategy is the gate, not confirmation.
Dining status known
Dining is confirmed, or the status is clearly flagged: pending cancellation, choosing between two, window not yet open. The build can proceed with pending dining as long as the status is explicit and accounted for in the timeline.
Multi-party flag checked
If another family or party has independent LL or dining booking authority, this is flagged at intake before the build starts. See Multi-Party Trips below. This affects LL strategy framing and Gate 2 requirements.
Library loaded New · v5.4
Before writing any ride or show copy, open library.json and confirm whether an entry exists for that ride. If it does, the body is used verbatim — no rewriting, no pulling from another client file. If it does not exist, the copy is written as a draft, flagged ⚠️ AI-generated, and held for Meredith review before the file closes. This is checked at Gate 1 so the intent is set before the build starts, not discovered at QA.

Gate 2: Ready to Deliver

All of the following must be confirmed before the file ships to the client.

LL bookings confirmed
Actual Lightning Lane booking confirmations received from the client. Times slotted into the timeline. If bookings differ from the planned strategy, the LL Conflict Tool is run before finalising the file. See Late Input Protocol below.
Conflict check complete
Any third-party itinerary (travel agent PDF, co-traveler's plan) has been cross-referenced against the build. All conflicts resolved deliberately before delivery. The LL Conflict Tool is the named mechanism for this step.
Library copy audit New · v5.4
Before delivery, verify every ride and show drawer in the file against library.json. For each ride: confirm the body matches the library entry verbatim. Any drawer that does not match is either (a) a missing library entry — flag for addition, or (b) drift from the library — correct to match. Any copy flagged ⚠️ AI-generated must have been reviewed and approved by Meredith before this gate passes. Client-specific context (e.g. rider swap, height flags, LL notes) is permitted alongside library copy — it is the ride description body that must be library-sourced.
Pre-Delivery QA passed
Full QA checklist run per Section 23. Drawer key verification complete. No orphaned drawer calls. Copy audit passed. File closes only after QA is done.
Tag audit: Top Pick + LL New · v5.3
Any timeline item carrying a Lightning Lane tag (tag-ll) must not also carry a Top Pick tag (tag-must). The LL tag already signals priority — Top Pick is redundant and creates visual noise. Before delivery, grep for tag-must.*tag-ll in the file. Count must be zero. Quick fix: sed -i 's/<span class="tag tag-must">⭐ Top Pick<\/span>\(<span class="tag tag-ll">\)/\1/g'. Top Pick is only used on non-LL items where a curation signal is needed (e.g. Kilimanjaro Safaris, Peter Pan rope drop).
Rope Drop Priorities item present New · v5.7
Every park day with a rope drop window must have a fixed "Rope Drop Priorities" timeline item placed immediately after the rope drop time-break label. Verify each park day before closing the file. The drawer must contain a ranked list of 2–4 first moves with brief reasoning per entry — not a generic list. See Timeline Items section for full pattern spec.

Build Order

The ideal sequence within the build. Internal order within each park day is not yet locked — pattern to be confirmed after first automated builds. What is confirmed: The Key tab is built first. QA runs last. Park days are built one at a time in chronological order.

The Key tab first
Build the Key tab (formerly Index) before any park day. The Your Trip at-a-glance card sets the structural truth of the file: people, hotel, park days, ticket type. Everything else is built against this foundation.
Park days in chronological order
One day at a time, in date order. Ideal sequence within each day: rides and dining in timeline order first, then LL bolts and drawer copy, then In-Park Intel, then entertainment and character meets if applicable. This sequence is the ideal case — it will not always hold. Treat it as a dependency map: LL bolts require confirmed strategy, dining items require confirmed reservations, character meets require confirmed priorities.
SOS and Key drawers last
SOS drawers and remaining Key tab drawers (LL, dining, rider swap, groceries, Baby and Toddler) are built after all park days are complete. They reference content across the full file and cannot be finalised until all days exist.
QA last
The Pre-Delivery QA checklist runs only after all park days, SOS drawers, and Key tab drawers are complete. Never run QA mid-build and treat it as done.

Multi-Party Trips

Intake flag
When a trip includes a second family or any party with independent LL or dining booking authority, capture this at intake. One question: "Is another family booking LL or dining for your group?" This is a rare case — typically a client who started with a travel agent before coming to The Park Edit.
Consequences for the build
If yes: (1) The LL strategy section must acknowledge both families and their respective selections. (2) Any third-party itinerary is a required Gate 2 input, not optional context. It must be collected and cross-referenced before delivery. (3) The multi-party flag is noted in the intake JSON so it surfaces in any future reference to this client file.

Late Input Protocol

Late inputs are expected
LL booking confirmations always arrive after the build has started — the booking window does not open until 7 days (on-property) or 3 days (off-property) before check-in. Late inputs are not a failure of process. The protocol is about handling them deliberately when they land, not preventing them.
Handling late inputs
When critical information arrives mid-build (LL confirmations, third-party PDF, dining change): run the LL Conflict Tool or a manual cross-reference against the current build state. Resolve all conflicts before continuing. Do not layer new information on top of an in-progress build without a deliberate reconciliation step. If the input requires structural rerouting, treat it as a Gate 2 task and complete the conflict check before reopening the timeline.
LL Conflict Tool
Internal tool at ll-conflict-tool.html. Upload the Park Edit HTML and either MDE screenshots or a typed list of actual LL bookings. Produces a conflict report with overview summary, per-ride conflict/gap/on-plan status, suggested routing fixes, and a numbered action list. Use at Gate 2 whenever actual LL bookings differ from the planned strategy.

Delivery Deadlines

Pre-Edit delivery
Must be delivered no later than 14 days before check-in. Absolute floor: one day before the LL booking window opens. The Pre-Edit contains the LL strategy — if it arrives after the booking window, the client is making selections blind. Calendar reminders for these deadlines are generated automatically from the intake form Key Dates card.
Park Edit delivery
Target: 2 days before check-in. This is fungible depending on when LL confirmations arrive — the file cannot close at Gate 2 until bookings are confirmed and the conflict check is run. Do not deliver before Gate 2 is complete.
